Privacy Policy

Last updated 27 October 2026.

Banana is made by Abrega, Inc. (“we”). This policy says what the app and this website do with information, in plain words. We are responsible for the information described here; write to support@abrega.com about any of it.

What stays with you

  • Your calendar. Banana reads and writes your Google Calendar from your device. Our server also reads connected calendars to provide event alerts and the web calendar, and to show invited guests the calendars you explicitly share (see below).
  • Apple Health. With your permission, Banana reads your workouts (with heart rate, energy, distance and routes), sleep, steps, active energy, exercise minutes, flights climbed, walking speed, heart rate, resting and walking heart rate, heart rate variability, breathing rate, VO2 max, weight, height, body fat, lean body mass and BMI. Banana saves workouts you record with it to Apple Health, with their routes, heart rate, energy, distance and stroke counts. With your yes, your iPhone keeps a copy of what it reads on our service, so your iPad and Mac can show it. Health data is not used for advertising. If you explicitly share a health section with a guest in the web app, that guest can view the information you approved. Earlier versions may have left health data in legacy iCloud storage; migration and removal of those copies are separate from service sync.
  • Trips. Trips Banana learns from (if you turn that on) stay on your iPhone for 60 days.
  • Without a Banana account, what you put into Banana stays on the device you put it in.

What Banana keeps for you

With a Banana account, we keep what you put into Banana on our service, so it is the same on all your devices and comes back on a new one:

  • your plans: routines, alarms, wake sounds, changes to a day, and when reminders come;
  • podcasts: the shows you follow, how far you got, what you listened to and, if you turn it on, where;
  • notes, with their recordings;
  • the address you chose for a name when inviting someone, so “Sam” is the same Sam next time;
  • your settings, with the places you saved;
  • your booking types and availability, including the phone number or in-person address you enter for a meeting place;
  • health data: meals and their photos, foods you saved or searched for, supplements and medication, workouts with their routes, and what Banana reads from Apple Health.

Health data is kept only with your yes; without it, it stays on the device it came from. Turning off Sync Health in Settings › Accounts & calendars withdraws your yes and deletes your health data from our service. It stays on your devices.

We keep earlier versions too, so a mistake can be undone. It is all stored with Cloudflare, encrypted on the way and where it is kept. No one at Abrega reads it. From it we count only what kind of thing changed and when (“a routine was edited”), never what it says; for health data, not whose.

Banana uses iCloud Keychain for your Google and Banana sign-ins and the address of your Google account. Earlier versions also used iCloud for app data. Legacy copies may remain while migration or cleanup is incomplete; turning off service sync does not by itself prove that earlier iCloud copies have been removed.

For a copy of all of it, write to support@abrega.com; there's no button for it yet.

What is worked out on your device, or by Apple

Transcripts are made on your device. Meal estimates, reading a food label, understanding an event you type (“Lunch with Sam Friday at 1”) and guessing where an event takes place from its title use Apple Intelligence: on your device, or, where your device can't, Apple's Private Cloud Compute, which works under Apple's privacy terms, keeps nothing and never shows us anything.

What needs the network

  • Food and supplements. A barcode Banana doesn't know is looked up in public databases (Open Food Facts, the NIH's Dietary Supplement Label Database and UPCitemdb): only the barcode is sent. Searching for a food or supplement by name sends the words you type to Open Food Facts or the NIH database.
  • Podcasts. Searching sends the words you type to Apple's podcast directory; episodes and transcripts come from their publishers. Devices of yours on the same network can pass downloaded episodes to each other directly.
  • Weather and travel. Forecasts come from Apple Weather, using your location. Travel times and place searches go to Apple Maps, with the places involved.
  • Guests. People you invite to an event are added to it in Google Calendar, which emails them as usual.
  • Speech model. If you choose the larger transcription model, it is downloaded once from Hugging Face.

Each of these services sees your device's network address when it answers, as any website does.

Your Banana account

Connecting Google also makes a Banana account, so a change to your calendar reaches all your devices within seconds, and your event alerts arrive even when Banana is closed. For it we keep:

  • which Google accounts you connected: their email address, Google’s id and the profile name Google supplies. These stay after you disconnect one, so that signing in with it again finds your account, and only it can delete the account;
  • Google's permission for your calendars (the access you granted Banana, which allows reading and changing events), stored encrypted with the key kept apart from the data. We use it only to ask Google to tell us when the calendars you chose change, to read those calendars for event alerts, and to give it back to Google when you delete your account;
  • the ids of the calendars being watched;
  • for event alerts, for each event on those calendars: its start and end, whether it was cancelled, your reply to it, and a scrambled fingerprint of its title (not the title), so a change can be told from a reply;
  • for each alert still to come: the event's title and time and the alert's words, which can name who invited you or changed the event. These go a day after the alert is sent or dismissed;
  • your alert times and your time zone, so alerts come when you set them;
  • which of your devices to notify: a random id for each, its kind, and the address Apple gives it for notifications;
  • a log of account events (connected, disconnected, deleted, Google refused) with their time.

When a calendar changes, Google tells us only that it changed. We pass that on to your devices through Apple's notification service, and read what changed from Google to decide which alerts to send. An alert reaches your devices through Apple's notification service, with the event's title and time. For the web calendar and approved guest views, our server also fetches event details from Google for that request. Those request-time details are not retained as a calendar history on our service.

If Google stops letting Banana in (you removed its access, or your Google account changed or closed), we notice within a day: we forget Google's permission, stop watching, and sign your devices out of the account. We don't delete it: it waits for you to sign in again.

Delete the account in Settings › Accounts & calendars. To prove it's you, Banana asks you to sign in with Google first. Then we stop watching, give Google's permission back to Google, and delete everything we hold about you, what Banana keeps for you and usage counts included. Copies stay up to 30 days in Cloudflare's recovery and trash, then they're gone. We keep only a line saying that an anonymous account was deleted and when, with no email, Google id or device in it.

Usage counts

To know whether Banana is working for people, the app counts a few things on your device and, now and then, sends us the counts: that the app was opened on a day, how far the first run got, which features were used (for example “a meal was logged by scanning a barcode”), and what failed. Each batch also says the kind of device, the app's version, where it was installed from (App Store or TestFlight), whether a calendar is connected, and the week the app was first opened.

Once a day it also counts how the app is set up, each fact on its own and numbers as ranges: which parts of Banana are switched on, how many Google accounts and calendars are connected, whether busy blocks are on, which permissions are granted, whether a Watch is paired, whether Apple Intelligence is available, roughly how many routines, podcasts and notes there are, the system version, region and language, text size and light or dark mode.

With a Banana account, these counts are also kept per account, so we can see how the app works for each person over time. Without one, we keep only daily totals across everyone.

Never: what you logged, any calendar, health, meal, place or note content. We don't store your IP address. You can choose whether to share optional usage counts in Settings › About › Share usage. Turning it off stops usage and diagnostic uploads.

Earlier versions could send bucketed counts of recent meals, supplements and saved foods. The service no longer accepts those inventory counts. Previously retained counts follow the analytics retention described below; updating the app does not itself delete historical entries.

Crash and performance reports

When Banana crashes, freezes, or uses too much battery or storage, your device's system (Apple's MetricKit) gives the app a report about it, and once a day a summary of how the app performed: launch and response times, battery, memory and storage use. The app sends these to us so we can find and fix problems. A report holds where in Banana's own code it happened, the timings, the device model, system version and region setting, and Banana's version. It never says who you are or which account, and holds nothing you logged. We keep reports for 90 days. They follow the same switch as usage counts.

Email in the separate Banana Beta app

Email is unavailable in the main Banana app and its public TestFlight build. The separate Banana Beta app can ask for additional Google permission to read and modify Gmail. It reads message headers, bodies and labels directly from Google and keeps a local mailbox index and downloaded content on that device. Mail credentials use a separate device-only Keychain entry, without iCloud Keychain synchronization. This mailbox is not uploaded to Banana’s account-sync service or exposed through guest sharing.

Implemented mailbox changes, such as marking read, changing labels, archiving or moving to Trash, are sent to Google. In the separate Beta app, changes require an account marked as a test account. Live composing and sending are unavailable. You can revoke Banana’s Google access from your Google account permissions; revoking remote access does not itself erase copies already downloaded to a device.

Google user data

Banana's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

  • What Banana reads: your list of calendars and their events; Gmail data only in the separate Beta app after its additional permission, as described above.
  • What Banana writes: events you create or change in Banana, and, unless you turn it off, “Busy” blocks that copy the time (not the title or details) of a plan on one of your calendars to your other calendars.
  • Where it is kept: events are kept on your device to show your day. Our server retains what “Your Banana account” lists and the guest addresses you chose (“What Banana keeps for you”). It also fetches details for web-calendar and approved guest requests without retaining that event history.
  • What it is used for: showing and planning your day in the app, telling your devices when a calendar changed, and sending you alerts for your events. Event titles may be read by Apple Intelligence as described above, only to help you. Guest access is limited to the calendars and detail level you approve.
  • Never: used for advertising, sold, shared beyond the access you approve, read by people at Abrega, or used to develop, improve or train AI or machine-learning models.

Sharing with guests on the web

You choose which calendars and sections to share, preview the guest’s view, and then approve the invitation. Each calendar can show full details or only busy time. Food, Taken, workouts, health trends, notes and listening have separate permissions. Guests sign in with Google using the invited address and receive read-only access. Being invited by two people does not merge those people’s data or permissions.

We keep the guest address, chosen permissions, invitation and acceptance status, and delivery times. Our email provider sends the invitation. You can change permissions or revoke access from web sharing settings. Revocation stops further access through Banana; it cannot remove information a guest has already copied or saved.

Booking a time with someone who uses Banana

  • What you give. On a Banana booking page (book.bananatheapp.com) you give your name and email, and a phone number or a note if you add one. They go into the meeting on that person's Google Calendar, and we keep them to send your confirmation and reminders and to let you change or cancel.
  • Remembering you. That browser remembers your details so you don't type them again. If you sign in with Google or with a code we email you, booking pages also show your upcoming times with anyone who uses Banana.
  • Only times you're free. If you ask for it, Google tells us when your calendar is free or busy, without what your events are. We keep Google's permission so the next booking page can check without asking, and use it only while you're on a booking page.
  • Forget me, at the foot of every booking page, deletes what we keep about you and Google's permission. Your meetings stay on the other person's calendar.

Who helps us

Our server and this website run on Cloudflare, which also sends booking emails and processes all of this for us under its data processing terms; information there is kept mainly in the United States and the European Union, and may be handled in other countries. Apple provides iCloud Keychain, notifications, Apple Weather, Apple Maps and Private Cloud Compute, and Google provides Google Calendar, each under their own privacy terms. We also disclose the information you explicitly approve to invited guests, as described above.

How long we keep it

  • Your Banana account and its usage counts: until you delete the account, including while it isn't connected to Google.
  • Crash and performance reports: 90 days.
  • An alert's title and words: until a day after it is sent or dismissed. What we know of an event's times and reply: while its calendar is watched; it goes when you stop watching the calendar, disconnect that Google account or delete your account.
  • Daily totals across everyone: kept, as they say nothing about any one person.
  • What Banana keeps for you, with its earlier versions: until you delete your account, and health data until you withdraw your yes. Copies then stay up to 30 days in Cloudflare's recovery and trash.
  • Booking with someone: a booking's details until 30 days after the meeting; your remembered details and Google's permission until you choose Forget me, or after two years without use.
  • On your devices: until you delete it there.

Why we use it

We use the account, Google's permission and what Banana keeps for you to provide the app you asked for, and health data only with your explicit yes. We use what you give on a booking page to book the time you asked for, and Google's free and busy times only with your permission. We use usage counts and crash and performance reports because we have a legitimate interest in knowing Banana works. You can withdraw your yes, or turn the counts off, at any time.

No ads, no selling

Banana has no advertising and does not sell your information. Information is disclosed to service providers needed to operate Banana and to guests only as described in this policy.

Your choices and rights

  • Turn off usage counts and crash and performance reports in Settings › About.
  • Withdraw your yes for health data: turn off Sync Health in Settings › Accounts & calendars.
  • Delete your Banana account in Settings › Accounts & calendars.
  • Disconnect Google in Settings › Accounts & calendars, and remove Banana's access at myaccount.google.com/permissions.
  • Deleting the app removes what's on that device.
  • You can ask us for a copy of what we hold about you, to correct it, to delete it or to stop using it, at support@abrega.com. You can also complain to your data protection authority.

This website

Cloudflare serves bananatheapp.com and handles your network address to deliver the page. The website uses Cloudflare Web Analytics to measure page views and page-load performance. Its analytics beacon may be added by Cloudflare when a page is served. This measurement is separate from the app usage information described above; it does not read your calendars, meals, health records or account content.

Children

Banana isn't directed at children under 13.

Changes and contact

If this policy changes, the new version will be here with its date. Questions: support@abrega.com.
